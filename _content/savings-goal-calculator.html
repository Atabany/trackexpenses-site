How it is calculated

Still to save = goal − already saved. With a date, the calculator divides that by the days from today to the date: per day = still to save ÷ days. A week is seven of those days, and a month is the daily amount times 365 ÷ 12, so a goal 365 days away comes to one twelfth a month. In the example, 5,000 less the 500 already saved leaves 4,500; over 365 days that is 12.33 a day, 86.30 a week and 375.00 a month.

With a monthly amount, months needed = still to save ÷ monthly amount, rounded up. 4,500 at 300 a month takes 15 months; the last month completes the goal.

The calculator does not add interest. For a goal a year or two away, interest on an ordinary savings account changes the monthly figure very little, and leaving it out means you arrive slightly early instead of slightly short. For goals many years away, or money that is invested, the result here is the most you would need to put in, not a forecast.

Make the monthly amount real

A monthly figure works when it leaves your account before you can spend it. Three things help:

  • Move it on payday. Transfer the amount to a separate savings account the day you are paid, and budget the month on what is left.
  • Check it against your pay. If the figure is more than you can spare, move the date instead of giving up on the goal: twice the time is half the monthly amount.
  • Keep goals apart. An emergency fund, a trip and a new phone are easier to follow as separate amounts than as one balance.

In a 50/30/20 budget, savings are the 20%. If one goal needs more than that share of your take-home pay, you can see at once that either the date or another part of the budget has to change.

Questions

How much should I save each month to reach my goal?

Subtract what you already have from the goal and divide by the number of months left. To save 4,500 in 12 months you need 375 a month; in 24 months, 187.50 a month.

Should a savings goal calculation include interest?

For a goal one or two years away it makes little difference, and leaving interest out is the safer estimate. This calculator leaves it out, so any interest you earn brings the goal a little closer.

Where should the money for a savings goal come from in a budget?

Treat it like a bill you pay to yourself on payday. In a 50/30/20 budget it is part of the 20% for savings. If the goal needs more, lengthen the time or reduce another part of the budget.
