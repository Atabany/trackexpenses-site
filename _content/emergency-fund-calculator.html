Emergency fund calculator

An emergency fund target is your essential monthly costs times the months you want covered: 2,000 a month for six months is 12,000. Enter your costs, the months, what you have saved and what you can add each month to see the gap and the month you would close it.

How it is calculated

Target = essential costs per month × months of cover. Still to save = target − saved so far. Your savings also count as months: 3,000 against costs of 2,000 a month covers 1.5 months today.

Months to get there = still to save ÷ what you add each month, rounded up, because the last month is the one that completes it. 9,000 at 500 a month takes 18 months. The month shown assumes you add the amount once a month, starting next month, so 18 payments end 18 months from now.

The calculator adds no interest. Money for emergencies belongs somewhere safe and easy to reach, where interest is small; leaving it out means you arrive a little early rather than a little short. It also assumes your costs stay where they are, so recheck the target when your rent or your household changes.

Three months, six or twelve?

There is no single right number; it depends on how quickly your income could come back and how many people depend on it.

  • Three months suits a steady job, a second income in the household and no one else relying on you.
  • Six months is the common middle: one income, or a job that would take a while to replace.
  • Nine to twelve months suits freelance or commission income, a single income with children, or work in a field where jobs are scarce.

Count only essentials, not your whole budget: in an emergency the eating out and the subscriptions stop. Building it slowly still helps, because one month of cover already turns a broken car or a sudden bill into an inconvenience instead of a debt.

Questions

How much should my emergency fund be?

A common target is three to six months of essential costs: rent or mortgage, bills, groceries, transport, insurance and minimum debt payments. With an irregular income or people who depend on you, aim nearer nine to twelve months.

Should I count my whole budget or only essentials?

Only essentials. In an emergency you would stop eating out, shopping and most subscriptions, so the fund needs to cover what you could not stop.

Does the calculator include interest?

No. Emergency money is best kept safe and easy to reach, where interest is small, so the result is the most you would need to put in. Any interest you earn brings the date a little closer.

Should I build an emergency fund before paying off debt?

Many people first save a small fund, about one month of essentials, so a surprise bill does not go on a card, then pay down expensive debt, then build the full fund. Keep paying at least the minimum on every debt throughout.
