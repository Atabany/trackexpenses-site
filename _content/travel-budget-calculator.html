How it is calculated

Per day from today = (trip budget − spent so far) ÷ days left. Days left count today and the last day of the trip. In the example, a 10-day trip with 4 days over has 6 left; 2,000 less the 900 spent leaves 1,100, which is 183.33 a day.

Even pace by today = trip budget × days over ÷ days in the trip. After 4 of 10 days, an even pace has spent 800 of 2,000. Spending 900 is 100 over that pace, which is why each day left gets 183.33 instead of the 200 a day planned at the start. When nothing is left, the daily figure is zero and the calculator says by how much the budget was passed.

In local money = the home figure ÷ the rate, where the rate is what 1 unit of local money costs in your home money. At 1 EUR = 1.10, 183.33 is 166.67 EUR. Use the rate your card or exchange actually gives you, fees included, not the rate in the news: that is the one your spending is really converted at. The display rounds to two decimal places, or to the local currency's usual decimals when you enter a currency code such as JPY.

Keeping to it on the road

  • Set the big costs aside first. If the hotel is paid at check-out, take it out of the budget before you start, or the first days will look richer than they are.
  • Check the daily figure each morning, before the day's spending. An expensive day is fine; the figure for the days after it simply goes down.
  • Keep cash in the count. Note each cash purchase, or count the money as spent when you take it out. Doing neither is how a trip budget drifts without anyone noticing.

How TrackExpenses does this

TrackExpenses records a purchase in the currency you paid in. Add the trip's currency under More → Additional Currencies; on a new entry, choose it and type the rate to your main currency (the field is filled in with the last rate you used for it). The entry then counts in your main currency in every total and budget. Entries in other currencies are free.

For the daily figure, give the trip a category of its own, such as Travel (More → Expense Categories), and a budget for it in More → Budgets. With Pro, tap the Today card at the top of your daily list, then Daily budget for, choose Chosen categories and tick Travel; switch on Stop on a date to end it on the trip's last day. The app then shares what is left of that budget, after its own repeating bills, across the days left, today included, worked out before today's spending so the figure holds still all day. The last day has to fall inside the current budget period, and when it passes, the daily budget goes back to the whole budget by itself. Your budgets do not change, and widgets and Siri keep to the whole budget.

Budget alerts, a free switch at the top of More → Budgets, tell you when the Travel budget passes 80% ahead of its pace and when it goes over.

Questions

How do I work out a daily budget for a trip?

Subtract what you have spent from the trip budget and divide what is left by the days left, today included. 1,100 left with 6 days to go is 183.33 a day.

Should flights and the hotel be in the travel budget?

Leave out what is already paid, from the budget and from the spending, so the daily figure is only about money you can still decide on. A hotel paid at the end of the stay belongs in the budget, or set it aside before you start.

Which exchange rate should I use?

The rate your card or exchange really gives you, fees included. Divide what a purchase cost in your home money by what it cost in local money to find it, and use that rate for the rest of the trip.

What if I have already spent the whole trip budget?

Then there is nothing left to share across the days that remain, and the calculator shows zero a day and how far over you are. Decide on purpose where the extra money comes from, instead of letting it come out of next month.
