Choose an iPhone expense tracker by workflow.

The best expense tracker for you is the one whose entry method you will keep using. Compare how purchases get in, how the month works, which languages and devices you need, and where your data goes before choosing by a feature count.

If your bank sends useful SMS or email alerts

None of the seven apps above documents reading bank SMS or email alerts on iPhone. TrackExpenses does it through a Shortcuts automation you create: the app never reads your inbox, it receives one matching alert at a time. Two smaller apps also document iOS message workflows, checked on 5 October 2026: Monetal documents SMS and email recognition, offline operation and no registration, and Tab.it documents an SMS Shortcuts setup that uses a token tied to a Tab.it account. None of these workflows proves that your bank’s format will work: test a real alert before relying on it. If your bank only sends app notifications, no iPhone automation can read them; pasting the alert is the way in.

If you want your bank connected

YNAB, PocketGuard and Copilot Money are built around bank connections, in the countries they list, and Spendee and Wallet offer them with Premium. TrackExpenses has no bank connection at all, by design. If automatic bank feeds are what you need, one of those will suit you better.

If payday is your month boundary

TrackExpenses, Money Manager and Monefy document a month that starts on a day you choose; Spendee documents a budget start date. YNAB documents that it has no such setting. A date option alone doesn’t mean every list and chart follows it: TrackExpenses applies its month to the ledger, budgets, charts and the daily budget.

If you spend in several currencies

Money Manager, Spendee, Wallet and Monefy list multiple currencies. YNAB doesn’t convert currencies within a plan and suggests one plan per currency. TrackExpenses keeps each entry in the currency you paid, at your own rate, and converts every total to one main currency.

If you want Arabic, Spanish or Portuguese

Of the seven, only Wallet ships Arabic; YNAB, PocketGuard and Copilot Money are English only. TrackExpenses is in English, Arabic with a right-to-left layout, German, Latin American Spanish and Brazilian Portuguese, and its voice entry, bank-alert reader and money coach work in all five.

If you prefer to say purchases

Compare supported languages, device requirements and whether audio is processed locally or remotely. TrackExpenses makes drafts on the device from a whole sentence, several purchases at once (10 saved entries free a month, unlimited with Pro). Spendee and YNAB document Siri actions, and Cash Book documented voice logging through a relay to an AI model when checked on 5 October 2026.

If you share money with someone

YNAB shares one budget with up to 6 people. TrackExpenses syncs between the devices on one Apple Account and has no shared budgets, so a couple keeping one budget together will be better served elsewhere.

Try a small real ledger

Add one expense, one income and a transfer between accounts. Check a payday boundary, export a sample and read the privacy policy. Those actions show whether the workflow fits you better than any claim that one app is “best” for everyone.
