# How to set a daily spending limit

A daily spending limit is the money left in your budget divided by the days left until the budget period ends, today included: 1,800 left with 15 days to go is 120 a day. Take out the bills still due before you divide, or the limit looks bigger than it really is.

## Work it out on iPhone in a minute

You need four figures for the same period: the budget, what you have spent so far, the bills still to come before the period ends, and the number of days left. Use one currency and one period for all of them. If your month runs from payday on the 25th, count from the 25th, not from the 1st.

- Money left = budget − spent so far − bills still due.
- Days left = every day from today to the last day of the period, today included.
- Daily limit = money left ÷ days left, rounded down.

Calculator on iPhone is enough, or use the free [daily budget calculator](../tools/daily-budget.html), which lets you subtract commitments before it divides.

Here is a worked example. Your monthly budget is 4,000 for 25 October to 24 November. On the morning of 10 November you have spent 1,800, and a phone bill of 150 and a gym renewal of 250 are still to come. Money left is 4,000 − 1,800 − 400 = 1,800. From 10 to 24 November is 15 days. 1,800 ÷ 15 = 120 a day.

## Why bills come out first and today counts

Skip the bills and you would divide 2,200 by 15 and get 146.66 a day. Spend that every day and the phone bill and the gym arrive with nothing left to pay them. A bill you know is coming is not money you can spend, so it leaves the pot before the pot is shared.

Today counts because you can still spend today. Dividing by 14 instead of 15 would hand you 128.57 a day and leave the last day of the period empty. Rounding down does the same job in small: a limit that is a fraction too low never runs out early.

## Work it out once a day, not after every purchase

Set the limit in the morning from what was left when the day began, then let today's purchases count against it. If you recalculate after every coffee, the limit drifts all day and stops being a number you can plan with.

The next morning the arithmetic moves on by itself. Spend 90 of your 120 today and tomorrow's limit is (1,800 − 90) ÷ 14 = 122.14. Spend 200 and it drops to 114.28. Underspending raises the days ahead a little, overspending lowers them, and nobody has to make up a lost day by hand.

## Keep the limit on screen in TrackExpenses

Set a budget under More → Budgets: the Total budget is the one the daily figure shares. Your budget month follows the start day in More → Period & Entry, so a payday month works the same way. Enter rent, phone and subscriptions as rules under More → Recurring so the app knows which bills are coming; two rules are free, more come with Pro.

The Daily list then opens with a Today card: your daily budget, what you have spent today and what is left today, with a bar. Tap it for the working, line by line: the Monthly budget, minus Repeating bills (paid already or still to come this period), minus spending before today, gives what was Left when today began, divided by the Days left, including today. The result is rounded down in your currency's precision and holds still all day. Under it, the sheet tells you what tomorrow's figure will be if you spend nothing more.

You can also ask Siri "What can I spend today in TrackExpenses", or keep the Today widget on your Lock Screen; the [Siri and widgets guide](log-expenses-with-siri.html) shows both.

The daily figure is part of Pro. Budgets, the pacing bars and the spent and left amounts are free, and when the budget is used up the app says so for free, with how far over you are and the days left, because that is a basic fact about your budget.

## A daily limit for one category or a trip

Sometimes the whole budget is the wrong pot. With Pro, tap the Today card, then Daily budget for, and choose Chosen categories. Only categories with a budget of their own are listed; tick one, such as Groceries, or several, such as Food and Transport, and they are read as one daily budget.

Turn on Stop on a date and pick a Last day inside the current budget period, and what is left is shared across the days up to that date instead of to the end of the period. That suits a trip: Travel with 3,000 left and ten days to go is 300 a day. The day after the last day, the daily budget is your whole budget again. Your budgets don't change, and widgets and Siri keep to the whole budget.

Stats → Budget also shows a daily amount on each category budget with Pro, with that category's own repeating bills taken out first. A category that holds only bills shows none. The [budget planner page](../features/budget-planner.html) shows the screens.

## What it doesn't do

The daily limit is a budget reference, not a bank balance or a card limit. It doesn't stop a payment, and it only knows what you have recorded: a bill you never entered as a rule or an expense is not taken out. There is no daily amount to type in by hand either; change the period's budget and the daily figure follows, so the two can never disagree.

The budget is kept in one period length, a week, a month or a year, and a period starts on a fixed day. There is no every-two-weeks budget period, so pay every other Friday needs a monthly or weekly convention you stick to.

## Frequently asked questions

Should I count today in the days left? Yes. You can still spend today, so leaving it out gives every remaining day a little too much and leaves nothing for the last one.

Why did my daily limit go down overnight? You spent more than the limit yesterday, so less is left to share across the days that remain. It also drops when a new bill is added that falls inside the period.

Do I need Pro for a daily spending limit? Not for the arithmetic: the calculator is free, and so are budgets and pacing in the app. The app's daily figure, the per-category daily amounts and the daily budget for chosen categories are part of Pro.

Can I set a fixed daily allowance instead? Not in TrackExpenses. The daily budget is always worked out from the period's budget, so lower or raise that budget to change it.
