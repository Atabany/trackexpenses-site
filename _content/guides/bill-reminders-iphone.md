# Get reminded before bills are due on iPhone

The Reminders app on iPhone can warn you before every bill: give each one a repeating reminder with its due date and an early reminder a day or two before. If you already track your bills in TrackExpenses, turn on Remind me the day before under More → Recurring and you get one notification at 9:00 the morning before each repeating expense is due, free.

## Set a repeating reminder in the Reminders app

Reminders is already on your iPhone and needs nothing else. For each bill:

- Open Reminders and tap New Reminder. Write the bill and its usual amount, such as "Rent 4,300".
- Tap the info button to open the reminder's details. Turn on Date and pick the next due date; turn on Time if you want a set hour.
- Tap Repeat and choose Monthly, or Every 3 Months for a quarterly bill.
- Tap Early Reminder and choose how long before, such as 2 days before. Then tap Done.

Each time the reminder comes round, tick it off and the next one is set from the repeat. Apple's [Reminders guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/add-details-iphec7a1de82/ios) shows the same steps with each button.

App Store subscriptions carry their own dates: Settings, then your name, then Subscriptions lists each one with its renewal date.

The Reminders route works, but it is a second list to keep. When the rent goes up, the reminder still says the old amount, and ticking a reminder doesn't mean the money was ready.

## Turn on bill reminders in TrackExpenses

If your bills are already in TrackExpenses as recurring rules, the reminders can come from the same list that writes them into your ledger. Open More → Recurring and tap + for each bill: choose Expense, the amount, the category and the account it is paid from, set Repeats to Every Month (the step beside it makes a quarterly bill every 3 months), and pick the next due date as the First occurrence. The [subscriptions and recurring bills guide](track-subscriptions-recurring-bills.html) covers the rules in detail. Two rules are free; Pro makes them unlimited.

Once there is a repeating expense, the Recurring screen opens with one switch: Remind me the day before. Turn it on and iOS asks whether TrackExpenses may send notifications. If you decline, the switch stays off; if you turn notifications off later in Settings, the screen says so and offers Open Settings.

With the switch on, a Next reminder row says when the next notification will arrive and which bill it is about. Bill reminders are free for everyone.

## What the reminder says

A notification arrives at 9:00 the day before the bill comes out, early enough to move money the same day. The time is shown the way your iPhone writes times.

For one bill, the title names it: "Rent is due tomorrow". The body gives the amount, the account it comes from and the day itself, "AED 4,300.00 from Main Bank · Tuesday, 1 December", because a notification read the next morning still says "tomorrow".

When several bills fall on the same day you get one notification, not several. The title says "2 bills are due tomorrow", and the body names them biggest first with the total in your main currency, such as "Internet and Gym · AED 600.00 in all". With more than three, it names the two biggest and how many more. Tapping a reminder opens the Recurring screen.

A worked example: you are paid on the 25th, rent of 4,300 leaves Main Bank on the 1st, and internet (350) and the gym (250) both renew on the 5th. On Monday 30 November at 9:00 you are told about the rent; on Friday 4 December, about internet and the gym together. Your salary rule sends nothing.

## Which bills get a reminder

- Repeating expenses above zero whose Active switch is on. A rule you have switched off sends nothing.
- Never income. A salary's real day is your employer's, and money arriving needs no warning.
- Bills that repeat weekly or less often. Rules that repeat more often than once a week, such as every day or every three days, are left out: a reminder every morning teaches you to ignore the one about the rent.
- The amount is in the rule's own currency, so a subscription billed in dollars is announced in dollars.

A reminder whose moment has passed is never sent late. Add a rule at lunchtime for a bill due tomorrow, and the 9:00 reminder for it has already gone by, so nothing arrives.

## What it doesn't do

You can't choose another hour or two days' notice, and there is no switch per bill; it is one setting for every repeating expense. If you need a longer warning for one bill, give that bill a reminder in the Reminders app as well.

Reminders are written up to 60 days ahead and topped up each time you open TrackExpenses. Leave the app closed for two months and they run out until you open it again.

The switch is a setting of each device. Your rules sync through iCloud, but an iPad gets reminders only if you turn the switch on there too, and with both on, both devices remind you.

A reminder announces the rule's amount. For a bill that changes, such as electricity, set the rule to a typical amount and edit the entry when the real bill arrives. TrackExpenses doesn't pay anything or check that a payment went through; a bank alert or the entry itself is your confirmation.

## Frequently asked questions

Can I be reminded two days before instead of one? Not in TrackExpenses: its reminders always come at 9:00 the day before. For a longer warning, set an Early Reminder on that bill in the Reminders app.

Why didn't I get a reminder for my salary? Income is never announced. Only repeating expenses get a reminder.

Why is a daily expense left out? Rules that repeat more often than once a week are skipped, so the reminders stay worth reading.

Are bill reminders free? Yes. The switch is free for everyone. The free ledger holds two recurring rules and Pro makes them unlimited, so every subscription can be reminded.

Will several bills on the same day send several notifications? No. Everything due on one day comes as one notification that names each bill and the total.
