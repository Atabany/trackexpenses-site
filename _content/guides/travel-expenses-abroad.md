# Track travel expenses abroad on iPhone

Record each purchase in the currency you paid, with the exchange rate your card or exchange office really used, and let one main currency carry every total. TrackExpenses does this in the free ledger: a main currency for totals, additional currencies for the trip, and your own rate on each entry.

## Start with what your iPhone already keeps

Wallet keeps a record for some cards. Open Wallet, tap a supported card and scroll down to see its recent activity; Wallet can also search by merchant, date, amount or location. It only covers that one card, only purchases its bank shares with Wallet, and never the cash you spent.

Calculator converts currencies too. Its Convert mode, and Math Notes, change euros or baht into your own money at a recent market rate. That is a good check at the till, but your card's rate usually includes the bank's margin and sometimes a separate foreign fee, so it is not the figure that leaves your account.

For a written log, a note per trip works: date, what, amount and currency on each line. Numbers is better for totals, with a table of Date, What, Amount, Currency and Rate columns and a formula for Amount × Rate. Its Travel Planner template, under Personal when you tap Choose a Template, is a start. The weak points are the same everywhere: a total per currency done by hand, and cash forgotten by day three.

## Decide which rate you will use

The true rate is the one in your own currency on your bank's alert or statement. If a 50.00 euro dinner shows as 213.50 on your card, the rate was 4.27, fees included. Use that figure for the rest of the trip's card purchases and correct it if a later alert shows the rate moved.

Cash has its own rate. If you changed 1,000 into 230 euros at the airport, each euro cost 4.35, and that is the rate for everything you pay from that cash. A separate fee for using the card abroad is an expense of its own, so log it as one line rather than hiding it in a rate.

## Set up TrackExpenses before you fly

Check More → Main Currency: every total, budget and account balance is reported in it. Then open More → Additional Currencies and add the currencies of the trip, such as EUR and THB. Multi-currency is free.

If you will carry foreign cash, add an account in that currency under Accounts and record the exchange as a transfer from your bank account into it, so the cash isn't counted as spending twice. The [cash expenses guide](track-cash-expenses.html) walks through it. The free ledger holds three accounts; more come with Pro.

For a trip budget, add a Travel category under More → Expense Categories and give it a budget under More → Budgets.

## Log each purchase in the currency you paid

In a new entry, type the amount, then tap the Currency row and choose the trip's currency. The row appears once you have more than one currency. A Rate to AED row follows (your main currency's code), filled with the last rate you used for that currency. A foreign entry can't be saved without a rate: saved at 1, a 45 euro taxi would be counted as AED 45, and that currency's history would be wrong from then on.

Each entry keeps the rate it was saved with, and every total converts at those rates, so a trip in three currencies still adds up to one figure. Accounts kept in a foreign currency are converted the same way, and the Accounts screen says so.

Voice entry hears the currency too. Say "taxi 18 euros" and the draft shows the Currency and the rate, prefilled, before you save. Ten saved voice entries a month are free, and the [voice guide](voice-expenses-iphone.html) covers devices and languages.

Bank alerts from abroad go through the same rules. When the alert prints the amount in your own currency beside the foreign one, as in "EUR 50.00 (AED 213.50)", the entry imports at what your bank charged. Otherwise it imports at the last rate you used for that currency and is flagged so you can check the rate, or it waits for a rate if you have never used one. A foreign row is never saved at 1.

## A daily budget for the trip

With Pro, the daily budget can follow the trip instead of the whole month. Tap the Today card at the top of the Daily list, then Daily budget for, choose Chosen categories and tick Travel. Turn on Stop on a date and pick the trip's last day as the Last day.

Say Travel has a budget of 6,000 and the flights took 1,500 before you left. On the first morning of a ten-day trip, 4,500 shared across ten days is 450 a day. Spend 380 today and tomorrow's figure rises a little; spend 600 and it drops. The working on the Today sheet counts the days up to your last day, today included. The day after the trip, the daily budget is your whole budget again, and your budgets never change.

The last day has to fall inside the current budget period. A trip that runs past payday into the next period can be planned only to the end of this one, then chosen again. Outside a trip, the [daily spending limit guide](daily-spending-limit.html) explains the same arithmetic for the whole budget.

## What it doesn't do

TrackExpenses doesn't fetch exchange rates from the internet. You enter the rate, prefilled from the last one, so totals match what your bank charged rather than a market rate it never gave you. If a card charge settles a few days later at a different figure, edit the entry's amount or rate.

It doesn't split a shared dinner between friends or keep a trip as a separate ledger. A trip is a category, a budget and, if you want, a cash account; after you come home, the Travel category's total in Stats is the trip's cost in your main currency.

## Frequently asked questions

Does TrackExpenses update exchange rates automatically? No. You enter the rate for each foreign entry, prefilled with the last rate you used for that currency, so the totals match what you really paid.

Should I record the foreign amount or the amount in my own currency? Record the amount you paid, in the currency you paid it, with the rate. Your main-currency total then follows, and the receipt and the ledger show the same number.

Is multi-currency a Pro feature? No. A main currency, additional currencies and your own rates are part of the free ledger. The daily budget for chosen categories up to a date is part of Pro.

How do I track foreign cash? Add an account in that currency, record the exchange as a transfer into it, and log each purchase from that account at the rate the cash cost you.
