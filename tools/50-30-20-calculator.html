How the calculator works

Each amount = take-home pay × its share ÷ 100. The per-year column multiplies one paycheck by the number of paychecks in a year: 12 when you are paid once a month, 24 twice a month, 26 every two weeks and 52 every week. The per-month column is the year divided by 12, so someone paid every two weeks sees a month slightly larger than two paychecks: 26 paychecks do not fit evenly into 12 months.

The display rounds to two decimal places. The three shares must add up to 100; if they do not, the calculator says what they add up to instead of guessing which one you meant to change.

What goes in each share

  • Needs are payments you would have to make even in a tight month: rent or mortgage, utilities, groceries, insurance, getting to work, childcare and the minimum payment on any loan or card.
  • Wants are the things you choose: eating out, delivery, shopping, entertainment, travel and most subscriptions.
  • Savings is money you keep: an emergency fund, investments, retirement, and loan payments above the minimum.

The split comes from the book All Your Worth by Elizabeth Warren and Amelia Warren Tyagi (2005), and it works on take-home pay. The 50/30/20 budget guide has a full worked example, with every category sorted into its group.

When 50/30/20 does not fit

Add up your needs first. If rent alone takes 40% of your pay, needs will not stop at 50%, and no calculator changes that. Enter the shares your month really has, such as 60/20/20, and treat 50/30/20 as the direction to move in when a lease or a loan ends. What matters is that the three shares are decided on purpose and add up to all of your pay.

From three numbers to a working budget

A split is a plan for the month; it helps only if you compare it with what you spent. In TrackExpenses, category budgets, pacing against today and moving money between budgets are free. Questions

Is the 50/30/20 rule based on income before or after tax?

After tax. Use your take-home pay: what reaches your account after tax and deductions. Where salaries are not taxed, that is the salary that arrives.

Where do debt payments go in a 50/30/20 budget?

The minimum payment on a loan or card is a need. Anything you pay above the minimum counts in the 20% for savings, because it reduces what you owe.

What if my needs are more than 50% of my pay?

Then the split has shown you the real constraint. Use shares that match your month for now, such as 60/20/20, and bring needs down when you can. Do not take the difference out of savings without deciding to.
