Budget pacing calculator

Compare how much of your budget is spent with how much of the period has passed. The calculator shows whether you are spending faster or slower than an even pace, where the period ends if nothing changes, and what you can spend per day from today.

The budget pacing formula

Budget spent = spending ÷ budget. Period passed = days completed ÷ days in the period. When the first share is larger than the second, you are spending faster than an even pace. The gap in money is spending − budget × period passed.

Days completed are the days before today, so on the first day nothing has passed yet. The days in the period include both its first and its last day. In the example, 25 September to 24 October is 30 days; at the start of 10 October, 15 are complete. Spending of 1,800 is 60% of 3,000, the period is 50% gone, an even pace would have spent 1,500, and the gap is 300.

Period ends at this pace = spending ÷ days completed × days in the period. 1,800 over 15 days is 120 a day; 30 such days come to 3,600, which is 600 over. Per day to stay within budget = what is left ÷ days left, today included. 1,200 over 15 days is 80 a day.

How to read the answer

An even pace is a reference line, not a rule. Rent, a loan payment or a yearly insurance bill paid in the first week puts anyone far over an even pace on day five, and nothing is wrong. Two ways to get a truer reading:

  • Pace only what varies. Take your fixed bills out of both figures: enter the budget without them and spending without them. What remains is groceries, transport, eating out and the rest of everyday spending, which is the part a pace can describe.
  • Look at the daily figure. If the amount per day for the rest of the period is one you can live on, an early bill has not hurt you. If it is far below what you normally spend in a day, the pace is telling you something real.

Being under pace is not automatically good news either: a bill that has not arrived yet is not money saved. Check what is still due before the period ends.

Questions

What is budget pacing?

Budget pacing compares the share of a budget already spent with the share of the period already passed. Spending 60% of the budget when half the period is gone means you are spending faster than an even pace.

What is the budget pacing formula?

Divide spending by the budget, and divide the days completed by the days in the period. The difference in money is spending minus the budget multiplied by the share of the period passed.

Is spending faster than an even pace always a problem?

No. Fixed bills paid early in the period, such as rent, put you over an even pace without any overspending. Leave fixed bills out of both figures to pace only everyday spending.
