Net worth calculator

Your net worth is everything you own minus everything you owe. List your cash, accounts and investments on one side and your cards, loans and mortgage on the other: the calculator adds up each side, subtracts, and shows the sum so you can check it.

How it is calculated

Net worth = what you own − what you owe. What you own is the sum of every amount in the first list; what you owe is the sum of the second. In the example, 300 + 2,500 + 6,000 + 4,000 = 12,800 owned, 1,200 + 3,000 = 4,200 owed, and 12,800 − 4,200 = 8,600. The result is worked out from the two totals, never added up separately, so the three figures always agree.

A net worth below zero means you owe more than you own. That is common while paying off a student loan or after a large purchase on credit, and it is a starting point, not a verdict: what matters is which way it moves from one month to the next.

What to count, and at what value

  • Own: cash, every bank and savings account, investments at today's value, and, if you want them in, a home or car at what they would sell for now, not what you paid.
  • Owe: the full balance on each card, even if you pay it off every month, and the amount still outstanding on every loan and mortgage, not the monthly payment.
  • Leave out what you cannot sell or would not count on, and money that is not yours, such as a card you only track for someone else.

Work it out on the same day each month, such as payday, so the comparison is fair. A home or car makes the figure larger but not easier to spend; many people also track the version with only cash, accounts and investments.

How TrackExpenses does this

The Accounts tab in TrackExpenses opens with your net worth, with Assets and Liabilities side by side beneath it. Card and Loan accounts count as liabilities; Cash, Bank, Savings, Investment and Other accounts count as assets. A card's balance owed shows as a positive figure under Liabilities, and a card paid more than it owed lowers that figure. An overdrawn cash or bank account stays under Assets as a negative balance rather than vanishing, which gives the same net worth as listing the overdraft as something owed, as this calculator does. Net worth is always Assets minus Liabilities, worked out from the two, so the three figures cannot disagree.

An account with Include in totals switched off keeps its own balance but stays out of the three figures, and accounts in another currency are shown in your main currency. Ask the money coach "what's my net worth?" and it answers on your phone from your accounts, without using an AI question. Three accounts are free; Pro makes them unlimited.

Questions

How do I calculate my net worth?

Add up everything you own, add up everything you owe, and subtract the second total from the first. 12,800 owned and 4,200 owed is a net worth of 8,600.

Should I include my home in my net worth?

You can, at what it would sell for today, with the mortgage under what you owe. Many people also track their net worth without the home, because that money cannot be spent without selling it.

Is a negative net worth bad?

It means you owe more than you own, which is common with a student loan or soon after buying a car on credit. What matters more is the direction: a figure that rises each month, even below zero, shows debt going down or savings going up.

Do credit cards count if I pay them in full every month?

Yes. Count the balance on the day you work it out. It is money already spent that the card will collect, so leaving it out makes your net worth look larger than it is.
