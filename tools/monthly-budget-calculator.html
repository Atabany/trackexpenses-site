Monthly budget calculator

To make a monthly budget, start from your take-home pay for the month and subtract every spending line and what you will save, until nothing is left to plan. Enter your pay and your lines below: the calculator shows the planned total, what is left to plan or how far over you are, and each line's share of your income.

How it is calculated

Monthly income = one paycheck × paychecks in a year ÷ 12. A year holds 52 paychecks for weekly pay, 26 every two weeks, 24 twice a month and 12 once a month, so the result is an average month. Paid every two weeks, most months bring two paychecks and two months of the year bring three, so the average is slightly more than two paychecks. To plan only on money that arrives every month, choose twice a month, which counts two paychecks, and give each third paycheck a job when it arrives.

Planned = every spending line + every saving line. Left to plan = monthly income − planned. When that figure is below zero, the plan is over your income by that much. Each line's share is its amount ÷ monthly income × 100, shown as a whole percent and rounded half away from zero, so 12.5% shows as 13%. Left to plan shows 100% less the planned share, so the two always add up to 100% on screen, however each one rounds. In the example, 3,310 of 4,000 is 82.75%, shown as 83%, and the 690 left is 17%.

Why aim for zero left to plan

A zero-based budget does not mean spending everything. It means every part of your pay has a job before the month starts, and saving is one of the jobs. Money left without a job tends to disappear into small purchases, which is why the calculator keeps it visible as its own figure.

  • Start with what you cannot move: rent or mortgage, bills, insurance, transport to work and minimum debt payments.
  • Then the flexible lines: groceries, eating out, shopping and subscriptions, at amounts your last two or three months support.
  • Give the rest a job: savings, an emergency fund or extra debt payments, until left to plan reaches zero.

If the plan comes out over, lower the flexible lines first. Want a rule of thumb instead of line by line? The 50/30/20 budget calculator splits the same pay into needs, wants and savings.

How TrackExpenses does this

In TrackExpenses, the salary plan answers the same question from your ledger: will this income cover everything you plan? It lists your income, a line for each spending category, Everything else, and a line for each Savings or Investment account under Saving & investing. Every planned line shows its share of income as a whole percent, rounded the same way as here, and the top of the plan shows Planned beside Left to plan, or Over by. Switch from Plan to Actual and each line shows what was spent or saved against its amount. A category's line is that category's budget, so the plan and your budgets never disagree.

The salary plan is part of Pro. Category budgets, pacing against today and moving money between budgets are free. The plan follows your budget period, which starts on a day of the month you choose, usually payday. There is no every-two-weeks period, so if you are paid every two weeks, plan the month.

Questions

What is a zero-based budget?

A budget in which take-home pay minus every planned spending and saving line comes to zero. Every part of your pay is given a job before the month starts, and saving counts as a job.

How do I make a monthly budget if I am paid every two weeks?

Multiply one paycheck by 26 and divide by 12 for the average month: 2,000 every two weeks is 4,333.33. Most months bring only two paychecks, 4,000, so a safer plan is built on two and gives each third paycheck a job of its own, such as savings.

Should savings count as a budget line?

Yes. Put savings in the plan as a line of its own, decided at the start of the month, instead of saving whatever is left at the end. In this calculator, mark it as Saving to see it apart from spending.

What if my budget comes out over my income?

Lower the flexible lines first, such as eating out, shopping and subscriptions, then look at the fixed ones when a contract ends. Do not plan to cover the gap from savings without deciding to.
