Start your budgeting month on payday

If you are paid on a fixed day each month, start your budgeting month on that day so income and the expenses it funds share one period. In TrackExpenses, More → Period & Entry changes the monthly start day for your ledger, budgets and charts together.

Use a manual method first

You can keep a payday budget in Numbers without installing a specialist app. Make a table with Date, Description, Income and Expense columns. Choose a start date and the day before your next monthly payday as the end date. Filter transactions to that window before adding income and spending. A calendar-month total will give a different answer whenever a purchase falls between the first of the month and payday.

The method works if you maintain the window consistently. The weakness is the repetition: changing the filter in one table but forgetting a chart or another sheet makes reports disagree. A monthly app setting is useful when it applies to the whole ledger rather than only to an individual budget. Before choosing any app, check whether its transaction lists, charts and comparisons follow the same dates.

Choose the period that matches your pay

Suppose salary normally arrives on the 25th. Your September pay period can run from 25 August through 24 September. A purchase on 24 September belongs to that period; a purchase on 25 September belongs to the next one. The label of a period is less important than its visible start and end dates.

TrackExpenses uses a fixed monthly start day. That is suited to monthly pay on the 25th or another regular date. It is not automatic scheduling of a paycheck every two weeks. If your pay moves substantially or you are paid several times each month, choose a reporting convention you can use consistently and keep the individual income dates accurate. Do not change the month setting repeatedly just to make one chart look better.

Days near the end of a month need a rule. A start day of 31 cannot literally occur in February. TrackExpenses clamps the chosen day to the last available day in a shorter month. If exact boundaries matter to you, browse the displayed dates after changing the setting and confirm that this convention fits the way you want to report.

Set the start day in TrackExpenses

Open More → Period & Entry and choose the monthly start day. Return to the ledger and check the period shown above the transactions. Browse the previous and next months so you can see where entries move. The records keep their original dates; you have changed the window used to group them.

Next, look at your budget and Stats for the same period. A whole-ledger setting is valuable precisely because the views should agree. If you are importing an older CSV, check its dates before interpreting your first payday total. A wrongly read date can put a correct amount in the wrong salary period and make the total seem inexplicable.

Separate the spending budget from account balance

A bank balance and a spending budget answer different questions. Your bank may contain money for rent, savings and a card bill as well as everyday spending. Decide which expenses belong inside the budget you are tracking, then log those expenses consistently. Do not treat the account's entire available balance as the amount you want to spend this month.

For an illustrative period budget of 1,200, spending of 480 leaves 720. That arithmetic does not guarantee that 720 is uncommitted cash: an unrecorded bill may still be due. TrackExpenses can show budget facts and, with Pro, planning calculations, but the quality of the result depends on the records and budget you provide.

Read budget pacing as well as totals

If 60% of a budget has been spent, the timing matters. Sixty percent spent after one week of a four-week period is a different pattern from 60% spent near the end. TrackExpenses pairs budget spending progress with a marker for where today falls in the period. Use the two together to notice when a purchase-heavy beginning is pulling spending forward.

Pacing is a description of recorded spending, not a judgment. A large planned bill early in the month can explain a high percentage perfectly well. Compare category totals and recurring commitments before deciding that your daily habits changed. Historical comparisons and basic budget facts remain free; forecasts, daily guidance and what-if planning are Pro features.

Do not count transfers as fresh spending

Moving money from your bank account to cash is a transfer between places you hold money. Buying lunch with that cash is an expense. Counting both as expenses doubles the apparent cost of lunch. The same issue appears when a credit-card purchase is recorded and the later card settlement is also treated as new spending.

Use transfers when moving money between your own tracked accounts, and check imported bank alerts carefully around card payments or cash withdrawals. An accurate period boundary cannot fix a misclassified transaction. Review a small sample of both income and spending when setting up your first payday month.

Keep a simple end-of-period review

At the end of the window, compare your recorded income, spending and the categories that changed. Export the selected month as CSV if you want a spreadsheet copy; that export is free. Keep a note of unusual events, such as annual insurance or travel, so you can explain the difference when comparing the next period.

The daily budget calculator can demonstrate remaining-budget arithmetic without sending your figures anywhere. Use the CSV guide when you need to review your own data outside the app.

Frequently asked questions

Is the custom month free? Yes. The month setting and ordinary budget tracking are part of the free ledger.

Will changing it change transaction dates? No. It changes how dated transactions are grouped into monthly periods.

Does it automatically follow biweekly paychecks? No. The setting is a fixed monthly start day.
