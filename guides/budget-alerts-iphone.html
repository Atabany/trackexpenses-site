Get an alert before you overspend

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated November 2026 · Suggest a correction

iPhone has no budget alert of its own, so the built-in options are your bank app's balance or spending alerts and a reminder to check in. TrackExpenses adds free budget alerts: a notification when a budget passes 80% while it is ahead of its pace, and another when it goes over, with what is left and the days it has to last.

What iPhone and your bank already offer

Many bank apps can notify you about a large card payment or a balance below an amount you choose; look in your bank app's notification or alert settings. These alerts watch an account, not a budget. A low-balance alert arrives when the money is nearly gone, and a large-payment alert says nothing about fifteen small ones.

Wallet shows recent activity on supported cards, but it has no spending limit to warn you about. The Reminders app can at least give you a habit: a reminder called "Check my spending" with Repeat set to Weekly, every Sunday evening.

A spreadsheet can colour a warning. In Numbers, a total that passes a figure can turn red with Conditional Highlighting. It only changes colour when you open the sheet, so it tells you after the fact rather than at the moment you spend.

Turn on budget alerts in TrackExpenses

Alerts need a budget to watch. Open More → Budgets and set a Total budget, category budgets, or both; Stats → Budget → Edit budgets opens the same screen. Once a budget exists, the screen shows a switch at the top: Budget alerts.

Turn it on and iOS asks whether TrackExpenses may send notifications. If notifications are later turned off in Settings, the screen says so and offers Open Settings. Budget alerts are free for everyone, like the budgets they watch, and the switch is a setting of each device.

When an alert comes

The app watches the whole budget and every category budget in the budget's own current period. Two moments are worth a notification.

Near: 80% spent, ahead of pace. Every budget bar in the app has a marker for where today falls in the period. An alert at 80% comes only when the bar is past that marker. Say Groceries has a budget of 1,000 for a 30-day month. On day 12 you reach 820: 82% spent with 40% of the month gone. You get "Groceries budget: 82% spent" and "AED 180.00 left for 19 days." Reach 850 on day 27 instead, and nothing arrives: 85% spent with 90% of the month gone is a budget working as planned. The budget pacing guide explains the marker.

Over: past the budget. When spending passes a budget, you get "Over the Food budget" and "AED 40.00 over, with 12 days left." A budget that jumps straight from below 80% to over gets the over alert only. For the whole budget the titles read "Your budget is 84% spent" and "You're over your budget". On the period's last day the body says what is left for today, or how far over you are.

Percentages are rounded down, so a budget is never called 80% before it is.

Repeating bills don't set off the 80% alert. Rent filling the Household budget on the 1st is the plan, not a warning, so a budget filled only by repeating bills never gets the near alert. It still gets the over alert if it goes past.

When several budgets cross a mark at the same moment, they arrive as one notification: "2 budgets need a look", with "Beauty: over and Fuel 89%" underneath. Up to four are named; past that, three and how many more.

Tapping an alert opens Stats → Budget.

Quiet by design

An alert you get every day is an alert you stop reading, so each one is said once.

  • A switch just turned on starts from where the budget stands. A category already at 90% is not news to you; the app speaks about what happens next.
  • Each mark is said once per period. A new period starts clean.
  • A mark is forgotten when the budget drops back under it, because money was moved in or an entry was deleted. Cross it again and you hear about it again. Time passing alone never repeats an alert.
  • Bank imports bring their alert with them. When a bank SMS automation files a purchase, the check runs before the import finishes, so the alert arrives with the purchase that caused it rather than the next time you open the app.

After the alert

Open Stats → Budget and look at the line. If one budget ran short while another has room, tap ••• on it and choose Move money in, or tap Cover on a line that is over. Moves apply to this period only, keep the total unchanged, and are free; next month starts from your usual budgets. The envelope budgeting guide shows the method.

With Pro, each category budget also shows a daily amount, so "AED 180.00 left for 19 days" becomes a figure for today. The budget planner page shows the screens.

What it doesn't do

The marks are fixed at 80% ahead of pace and over. You can't choose another percentage or switch alerts on for some categories only; it is one switch for every budget with an amount. Income, account balances and card limits are not watched.

An alert knows only what is in your ledger. Cash you haven't entered and bills you haven't added can't set one off, and lowering a budget below what you have already spent sends the over alert a moment after you save.

Frequently asked questions

Why didn't I get an alert at 80%? Either spending was behind the period's pace, the budget held only repeating bills, or it was already past 80% when you turned the switch on.

Can I change the 80% threshold? No. It is fixed at 80%, and only when spending is ahead of where today falls in the period, so a budget that is simply nearly finished near the end of the month stays quiet.

Are budget alerts free? Yes. Budgets, the pacing bars, moves between budgets and budget alerts are all free. Daily amounts per budget are part of Pro.

Will I get the same alert twice? Only if the budget drops back below the mark, for example after you move money in, and then crosses it again in the same period.

Do alerts arrive on my iPad too? Each device has its own switch. Turn it on where you want alerts.
