See how much you spent at one shop or on one thing

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated November 2026 · Suggest a correction

In this guide

To see how much you spent at one shop, search your records for its name and add up what comes back: Wallet and most banking apps can search a card's history, and TrackExpenses search adds up Spent and Income across every match in every period, free. For one kind of thing, such as coffee or fuel, a category does the same job.

Search your card history on iPhone

Wallet can search the activity on supported cards. Open Wallet, tap the search button and type the shop's name; Apple says you can also search by date, amount or location. It is quick for one card, but only for purchases that card's bank shares with Wallet, and never for cash or another card.

Your bank's app usually has a search box over its transactions too, and a statement downloaded as a spreadsheet can be totalled in Numbers with a SUMIF on the description column. Both stop at that one bank.

All of these meet the same snag: one shop appears under several names. The same supermarket can show up as CARREFOUR MOE, Carrefour City Centre and a payment terminal's code, so an exact search misses part of what you spent there.

Search everything in TrackExpenses

In TrackExpenses, open the Transactions tab and tap the magnifying glass. The search field asks for a payee, note, category or amount, and it looks through every period, not only the month you are viewing.

Type part of a shop's name, such as "carrefour". Search ignores capitals and accents, so it finds "Carrefour City Centre" and "CARREFOUR MOE" alike, along with any entry whose note or category contains the word. A line such as "23 matches across all periods" sits above the results, and under it two totals: Spent and Income.

The totals count every match in your ledger, not only the rows on screen. The list stops at a few hundred rows to stay quick, and when it does, a note says "Not every match is listed here. The totals count all of them."

Amounts are in your main currency, at the rate each entry was saved with, so purchases abroad add up correctly. Transfers between your own accounts appear in the list but in neither total, because moving money is not spending. Search and its totals are free.

You can also type an exact amount, such as 45.50, to find every entry of that amount: useful when a bank statement shows a figure you can't place.

Look at one thing instead of one shop

For a kind of spending rather than a shop, use its category. Typing a category name in search, such as "Fuel", lists every fuel entry from every period with its total.

To see one period, open Stats → Overview and tap the category. You get this period's total and entries, and a bar chart of its totals over the last six months. On the Transactions tab, the Description view groups the period by description instead, each with its total and the number of entries, biggest first; tap a name to see its entries.

Two quicker ways to ask about a category this period:

  • Siri. Say "How much did I spend on Groceries in TrackExpenses", with your own category name, and Siri answers with the spending against that category's budget.
  • The money coach. Type "How much is left for groceries?" and the coach answers on your phone, without using one of your AI questions. A question about a shop rather than a category goes to the AI model, after you agree, and uses one of your AI questions (ten a month are free). The model sees at most 20 matching purchases, so for a full total, search is the better tool.

Keep shop names consistent

Search can only add up what it can find, so a few habits make totals exact.

When you type a description, Previous descriptions suggests the names you have used, and picking one keeps the spelling the same. A shop you have used before also fills in its usual category and account, and the entry says "Category and account from your past Carrefour entries", so you can see where they came from.

Entries imported from bank alerts carry the merchant name as your bank prints it. Search for the part every version shares, such as "carrefour" rather than "Carrefour Mall of the Emirates".

For spending that isn't a shop, such as work lunches or a home repair, put a word in the note. Search reads notes too, so "repair" later finds every entry about it.

A worked example

Suppose your coffee entries sit under Food, described as "Starbucks" and "Costa". Search "starbucks": 31 matches, Spent 589.00. Search "costa": 12 matches, Spent 198.00. That is 787.00 on coffee across the whole ledger, about the price of a week's groceries.

Searching "coffee" would miss both, because the word is in neither description. If you want coffee as one figure every month, give it its own category, so Stats shows it without a search. To see what a daily habit like this costs over a year and five years, try the habit cost calculator.

What it doesn't do

Search totals cover your whole ledger; search has no date range. For one month, use the Description view or the category in Stats. Totals only include what you recorded: cash you didn't enter and purchases under a name you didn't search for are not in them.

The coach answers category questions on the phone, but not shop questions, which use an AI question. The reports feature page shows the views mentioned here.

Frequently asked questions

Can Wallet show how much I spent at one store? Wallet can search a supported card's activity by merchant. It covers only that card and the purchases its bank shares, and never cash.

Does TrackExpenses search add up what it finds? Yes. Under the results it shows Spent and Income for every match in every period, in your main currency. It is free.

How do I see what I spent at a shop in one month only? Open the Transactions tab for that month and switch to the Description view, which totals each description in the period.

Why doesn't search find a purchase I know I made? It may be spelled differently or sit under another name. Search for a shorter part of the name, the category, or the exact amount.
