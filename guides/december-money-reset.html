December Money Reset: A Spending Challenge You Can Keep

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated November 2026 · Suggest a correction

In this guide

  • The method, with a notebook or a spreadsheet
  • Doing it with the money coach in TrackExpenses

  • Frequently asked questions

A December money reset that lasts is one limit on one habit, not a ban on spending: find the everyday category that took the most of last month's money, set a limit about a fifth below it, and check where you stand once a week. TrackExpenses does the reading, suggests the limit and sends the weekly check-ins, but the method works on paper too.

Why one limit beats a no-spend month

December is the month of gifts, dinners and year-end sales, so "spend nothing" breaks in the first week. One limit on one category survives, because the rest of your month carries on and you watch one figure.

Pick the category by last month's facts, not by guilt. Coffee feels like the problem, but groceries, delivery or eating out usually move the month more.

Leave fixed bills out: rent and subscriptions are big, but cutting them is a decision you make once, not a habit.

The method, with a notebook or a spreadsheet

1. Read last month. Add up everything you spent in November, without rent and other repeating bills. Then add up each everyday category: food, groceries, transport, shopping. 2. Find the one that stands out. Look for a category with at least three purchases that took a real share of that everyday total, say 15% or more. One big purchase is a one-off, not a habit. 3. Set the limit. Take 80% of what that category cost in November and round it down to a figure you will remember. 960 becomes 750, not 768. 4. Check in once a week. Every Sunday, add up the category so far and compare it with where it should be: by the end of week two, about half the limit. Ahead of the pace means slow down. Over the limit means every day under from here still counts. 5. Close the month. On 1 January, write down what the category cost in December next to November's figure. That difference is what the reset saved.

A payday month works the same way. If your money month runs from the 25th, read the last full payday month and set the limit for the one running now.

Doing it with the money coach in TrackExpenses

Open the Coach tab, the one in the middle of the tab bar. Once a full month is logged, the coach opens with a reading of it: "Hi. I looked at your November.", the category that stood out ("Groceries was 31% of your everyday spending.") and a suggestion: "Try a limit of 750 for December. I'll check in every week." It skips repeating bills and one-off big purchases on its own, and a category needs at least three purchases and 15% of your everyday spending before the coach names it. If nothing stands out, it says so rather than inventing a target.

The suggestion is 80% of what the category took, rounded down to a round figure, with one step either side to pick from (700, 750 or 800 in the example below). The challenge card reads "December challenge: Groceries under 750". Tap Join.

Joining sets that category's budget for that month only, so the limit appears in Stats → Budget with its pacing bar and today notch like any other budget. The coach then checks in once a week with a notification titled "Your Groceries challenge" ("410 of 750 so far. Tap for this week's check-in."), and the check-in in the Coach tab says whether you are on pace, a little ahead of it or over. When the month ends, it tells you how it went: "You kept Groceries under 750 in December: 702." and how that compares with November.

Changed your mind? Stop this challenge puts the category's budget for that month back to what it was. Nothing you logged changes.

Reading the month and the suggestion are free. Joining a challenge and the weekly check-ins come with Pro. The AI money coach page covers the rest of the coach, including the questions you can type to it.

A worked example

Say your November everyday spending, without rent and subscriptions, was 3,000. Groceries took 960 of it over 14 trips to the shop, which is 32%. Nothing else came close.

  • 80% of 960 is 768, and the round figure below it is 750.
  • By Sunday 14 December you have spent 410. Two weeks in, the pace says about 340, so you are ahead by about 70. You plan two shops from the freezer that week.
  • By the 21st you are at 540, back on pace.
  • December ends at 702. That is 258 less than November.

When the challenge won't start, and other limits

Start early in the month. The coach offers a challenge for the month you are in only while less than a quarter of it has passed, so by about 7 December for a calendar month. After that it offers the challenge for January, so you don't start a target you can't meet.

It needs a whole month logged. With less than a full month in the ledger, the coach says "I'll have something to say once a full month is logged." Log purchases as they happen, by voice or from your bank's messages, and it reads that month as soon as it ends.

Gifts are not everyday spending. If December gifts would swamp the month, give them a category and a budget line of their own under More → Budgets, so they have a figure of their own and don't hide the habit the reset is about.

Weekly check-ins need weeks. A budget kept by the week gets one report at its end instead. With notifications off, check-ins wait in the Coach tab.

One challenge a month. The coach runs one challenge per budget month. If more than one habit needs work, take the biggest in December and the next in January. Budget alerts can watch the other categories meanwhile.

Frequently asked questions

Is a December money reset the same as a no-spend challenge? No. A no-spend challenge stops all optional spending for a set time. A money reset keeps your month as it is and puts one limit on the habit that cost the most last month, which is easier to keep through the holidays.

How much lower should the limit be? About a fifth lower than last month. 80% is a real change you will notice, without being so low that one family dinner breaks it.

Can I change the limit TrackExpenses suggests? Yes. The coach offers one step below and one above its suggestion, and after joining you can still change that category's budget like any other.

Does the coach send my spending anywhere? No. The reading, the suggested limit and the weekly check-ins are worked out on your iPhone from your own ledger. Only questions you type that need the coach's AI are sent, and the app asks your permission first.
