How to track expenses on iPhone

By Mohamed Elatabany · Updated November 2026 · Suggest a correction

To track expenses on iPhone, write down every purchase in one place on the day you make it, in a note, a Numbers table or an expense app, and total it by category once a week. The built-in apps cost nothing; an app such as TrackExpenses takes the typing off you with voice, bank alerts and Apple Pay automations.

Notes: the quickest start

Open Notes and start a note for the month. Write one line per purchase: the date, what it was and the amount, such as "12 Nov, groceries, 86.40". It takes seconds and works offline.

For the total, Calculator's Math Notes adds figures for you: type an expression with an equal sign, such as 86.40 + 12.50 + 31 =, and the answer appears. A note has no categories and no budget, though, so "how much went on food this month" means reading every line.

Numbers: totals and categories

Numbers is free on iPhone and turns a list into totals. Tap Choose a Template and look under Personal Finance: Simple Budget and Personal Budget are ready-made, or start from a blank table with Date, Description, Category and Amount columns. A SUMIF formula then totals each category, and a chart shows where the money went.

The cost is the typing. Opening a spreadsheet at the till, finding the next empty row and picking a category on a phone screen is where most spreadsheet budgets stop by week three.

Wallet: a record you already have

If you pay with Apple Pay, Wallet keeps part of the record for you. Tap a supported card and scroll to see its recent activity, or search by merchant, date or amount. It is a statement, not a budget: one card at a time, only what that card's bank shares, nothing in cash and no categories you choose.

An app: the ways an entry gets in

An expense app is worth it when it makes recording faster than forgetting. TrackExpenses works without a bank login and offers several routes into one ledger:

  • Typing. A keypad opens on the amount, then category, account and description. A shop you have used before fills in its usual category and account, arrows beside the date step a day at a time, and Save as template turns a repeat purchase into one tap from Quick Add.
  • Voice. Say "coffee 15, taxi 40 and lunch 60" and you get three drafts to check and save. Speech is processed on the device. Ten saved voice entries a month are free; Pro makes voice unlimited. See the voice guide.
  • Bank SMS. A Shortcuts automation passes each bank text to the app, which reads the amount, merchant and direction and skips codes and declined payments. Ten successful imports a month are free on each device. Start with the bank SMS guide.
  • Bank emails. If your bank emails instead, an Email automation does the same through Apple's Mail app: the bank email guide.
  • Apple Pay. A Wallet Transaction automation sends each Apple Pay purchase's amount and merchant: the Apple Pay guide.
  • Pasting. Copy a bank text or email alert, paste it into a new entry, and the amount, date and payee fill themselves in.
  • Cash. Cash leaves no alert, so treat it as an account: the cash guide.

There are shortcuts to all of this. Touch and hold the app icon for Say an expense, New expense, Paste bank message and Ask your coach, or use Siri and the widgets in the Siri and widgets guide.

How to choose a method

Choose by where your money actually goes, not by the cleverest feature.

  • Mostly card payments, and your bank texts or emails each one: an automation records them for you. Check one real alert in the app before relying on it.
  • Mostly cash or many small purchases: voice or templates, because you record at the till in a few seconds.
  • You don't want to share a bank login: a manual or alert-based app; the no-bank-connection guide compares the options.
  • You spend in several currencies: record each purchase in its own currency with your rate, as the travel guide shows.
  • You are paid on a fixed day: start your budget month on payday so income and spending share a period; see the payday guide.

Most people mix two: an automation for cards, and voice or typing for cash.

Make it a habit

Record a purchase when it happens; an entry made at the till is accurate, while one made at the weekend is a guess. If you forget, More → Daily Reminder sends a nudge to log the day at a time you pick.

Once a week, spend ten minutes on the list. Compare it with your bank's statement, fix a category or two, and delete anything recorded twice, such as a purchase that came by alert and was also typed. Then look at the budget: in TrackExpenses every budget bar has a marker for where today falls in the month, so you can see whether spending is running ahead of time, not just how much is gone.

What an app can't do

TrackExpenses doesn't connect to your bank or download your statements. Automations cover banks that send texts or emails; no iPhone automation can read a push notification, and Shortcuts shows its own banner when an automation runs. An entry is only as complete as the alerts and the cash you record, which is why the weekly check matters whichever method you choose.

Frequently asked questions

What is the easiest way to track expenses on iPhone? The one you keep doing. For most people that means an app that records card payments from bank alerts and takes cash by voice in a few seconds, checked once a week.

Can iPhone track my spending automatically? Not by itself. Wallet shows activity on supported cards, and Shortcuts automations can pass bank texts, emails and Apple Pay purchases to an app that records them.

Do I need to connect my bank account? No. A note, a Numbers sheet or an app such as TrackExpenses works from what you enter and from alerts your bank already sends you.

Is TrackExpenses free? The ledger is free: unlimited manual entries, budgets, search and private iCloud sync, with three accounts, two recurring rules, and ten voice entries and ten bank SMS imports a month. Pro adds unlimited voice and imports, more accounts and rules, planning tools and every export.
